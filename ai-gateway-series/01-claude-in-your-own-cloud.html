Claude is moving into your cloud. Who's minding the door?

What changes when AI access stops being a subscription and becomes infrastructure.

A pattern keeps coming up when I talk to enterprise teams: they want Claude, but through the cloud provider they already trust, under the contracts, networks and identity they already run, rather than as a stack of individual subscriptions. It makes sense. Procurement is simpler, data stays where policy says it should, and the spend lands on a bill somebody already manages.

It also quietly changes who is responsible for everything around the model.

What a subscription does without being asked

When people use a product sold per seat, a lot of governance arrives bundled with it: somebody is given a seat, usage is visible somewhere, and there's a ceiling. Move to a model hosted in your own cloud and the model is what you get. The seat list, the per-person usage, the spend limits and the rules about who may use which model become yours to build, or to go without.

Three jobs nobody assigned

  1. Access. Who may use which models, how do they get that access, and how does it end?
  2. Accountability. Who spent what, in money, and who is about to hit a wall?
  3. Assurance. Is it working, and when it isn't, where did it stop?

They're the same three questions every AI platform meets, and in a migration they show up on day one, usually from a finance or security team that wasn't part of the original project.

Why a gateway, and why a portal

A gateway in front of the models gives you one place to apply the rules. But rules nobody can see or change aren't governance, they're a black box. So the product I built has two halves: a gateway that decides each request, and a portal that explains and manages what the gateway does.

If you're moving access to a third-party provider you need both, and you need them to behave as one product. The day you buy them separately is the day they disagree about who a person is.

What people actually ask for

  • Sign in with the identity I already have.
  • Use the tools I already like: Claude Desktop, Claude Code, GitHub Copilot in VS Code, OpenAI Codex, or our own applications.
  • A budget that's real, in money, and visible to me.
  • A way to ask for more when I reach it.
  • Answers for finance without a data project.

What this isn't

It isn't an argument against subscriptions, or for any particular provider. Per-seat products are a perfectly good way to buy AI for plenty of teams. The point is narrower: when access moves into infrastructure you run, the controls that used to come with the seat have to come from somewhere else, and "nowhere" is the usual answer on day one.

The timing problem

Governance gets bolted on after the first surprise: the invoice nobody expected, the person who shouldn't have had access, the afternoon the service was down and nobody could say why. It is much cheaper to put the door in before the traffic arrives than to retrofit one into a building full of people.

The rest of this series is how I approached each of these, at the level of ideas: what problem each part solves and why it matters, with the implementation left out.

Moving the model into your cloud moves the governance onto you. Plan for it before the first invoice.
